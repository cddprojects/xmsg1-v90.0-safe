Writing a CV for Remote and Home-Based Roles

Last reviewed November 2026

An employer hiring remotely can't see you at work, so it looks for proof that you can deliver without supervision.

Put results first

Use short bullet points showing what you achieved on your own, such as tickets resolved, campaigns delivered or projects completed on schedule.

Highlight remote-friendly skills

  • Tools you already use: email, chat apps, spreadsheets, video calls and project boards.
  • Clear, concise and courteous writing.
  • Managing your own time and being reliable.

Spell out your availability

Give your working hours, your time zone and any core hours you can commit to. It matters for part-time and home-based applications alike.

Keep the layout simple

Stick to one or two pages with plain formatting and a brief summary at the top. Link to a portfolio if you have one.

Our work-from-home guide suggests roles to aim for.

Common questions

Is it worth stating my time zone?

Yes. Say where you are, the hours you work and how much overlap you can offer.

Is a portfolio necessary?

It helps for creative, technical and content roles, but not for every job.

Key takeaways

  1. Show results that you achieved by yourself.
  2. List the remote tools you already know.
  3. Give your time zone and availability clearly.
  4. One or two pages is enough.
