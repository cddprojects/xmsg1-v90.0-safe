How to Read a Job Ad for a Flexible Role

Last reviewed November 2026

You've read plenty of job ads. Ads for flexible roles follow their own patterns, and a few details matter more than the rest.

The details that tell you if it fits

  • Hours: fixed shifts, a roster, or a vague "flexible" with no figures.
  • Work mode: "WFH" might mean always at home or one day a week. Ask which.
  • Type of employment: employee, contract or freelance. This changes CPF, leave and tax.
  • How pay works: hourly, monthly, per task or by commission.

Read the pay wording carefully

"Up to" describes a ceiling, not a promise. Commission-only pay rests entirely on results. A range shown on MyCareersFuture hints at what the employer plans to pay. Ask what people in the role normally earn.

Fine print to double-check

  • Training bonds or clawback clauses.
  • How long probation lasts and what notice applies during it.
  • Exclusivity, or limits on other work if you have another job.
  • Who provides equipment and who pays for internet or travel.

Check who is hiring

Make sure the company is real and is the one recruiting. Prefer official channels such as MyCareersFuture or the company's careers page. Our glossary explains unfamiliar terms.

Common questions

How should I read an "up to" salary?

As the ceiling of a range, not an amount you can count on. Ask what people in the role usually earn.

What does probation mean?

A trial period at the start of a job, when the notice period may differ. Your contract will say.

Key takeaways

  1. Start with the hours, how pay is calculated, the work mode and the type of employment.
  2. A salary range, or an "up to" figure, is not guaranteed pay.
  3. Check the probation and notice periods before you apply.
  4. Verify the employer through an official source.
