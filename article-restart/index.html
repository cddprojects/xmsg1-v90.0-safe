Going Back to Work After a Career Break

Last reviewed November 2026

CV gaps are common, whether from caring for family, retrenchment, illness, study or simply a need to rest. Employers meet them more often than you might think.

Keep the explanation short and true

Two calm sentences will do: what happened, what you did or learned meanwhile, and what you want to do next.

Ease back in

Part-time or contract work helps you rebuild confidence, references and recent experience, without the pressure of going straight back to full-time.

Bring your profile up to date

  • Put recent skills, courses or volunteering on your CV.
  • Ask a past colleague for a short reference.
  • Browse SkillsFuture options for relevant skills.

Start at a pace that feels right for you. Nobody sets a deadline for beginning again.

Our job types guide suggests places to begin.

Common questions

How long is too long for a gap?

There is no set limit. Employers usually care more about what you can do today and how you account for the gap.

Is part-time work a good first step?

It can be. Part-time or contract work helps rebuild confidence and recent experience, and many people begin that way.

Key takeaways

  1. Gaps on a CV are common and easier to explain than most people expect.
  2. Keep the explanation brief and truthful.
  3. Part-time or contract work is a gentle way back in.
  4. Update your CV, references and skills.
