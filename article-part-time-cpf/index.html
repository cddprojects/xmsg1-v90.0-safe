Part-Time Work and CPF: What to Check on Your Payslip

Last reviewed November 2026

You already know CPF exists. For a part-time role, the real question is whether it is being paid, and paid correctly.

Who receives CPF contributions

CPF applies to employees who are Singapore Citizens or Permanent Residents, depending on age and monthly wages. Foreigners on work passes are generally not covered. Rates change, so check the CPF Board website.

What to look for on a payslip

  • Gross pay and the pay period.
  • Deductions, including your own CPF contribution where it applies.
  • The employer's CPF contribution, shown on its own line.
  • Net pay.

And for freelancers?

A freelancer is self-employed, so no employer pays CPF for them. Depending on income, self-employed people may have to contribute to MediSave.

Worth asking

  • Am I an employee under a contract of service?
  • Will CPF be paid every month?
  • Will I get an itemised payslip?

Our part-time jobs guide has more.

Common questions

Will part-timers receive an itemised payslip?

Employees should get one. MOM lists the current requirements.

Do freelancers receive CPF from their clients?

No, because freelancers are self-employed. CPF Board explains the MediSave obligations.

Key takeaways

  1. CPF applies to employees who are citizens or PRs, depending on age and pay.
  2. A payslip should list your pay and your CPF contributions.
  3. Freelancers are not employees, so no employer CPF is paid.
  4. Current rates are on the CPF Board website.
