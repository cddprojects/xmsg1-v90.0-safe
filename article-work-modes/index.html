Part-Time, Remote, Hybrid and Freelance: What the Terms Mean

Last reviewed November 2026

A job ad can blur three separate things: the number of hours, the place of work and your legal status. Pulling them apart is the first step.

Hours: part-time or full-time

The Part-Time Employment Regulations define a part-time employee as someone contracted for fewer than 35 hours a week. Part-timers remain employees with a contract, and many protections apply pro rata. See MOM for the current rules.

Place: remote, hybrid or on-site

  • Remote / WFH: you work away from the employer's premises, often at home.
  • Hybrid: a blend of both. Ask how many office days are required, and whether they are fixed.
  • On-site: you work at the employer's premises or a client's.

Status: employee, contractor or platform worker

An employee works under a contract of service. A freelancer or contractor works under a contract for services and arranges their own CPF and tax. Platform workers, such as ride-hail and delivery workers, fall under a separate framework in the Platform Workers Act.

Put these to any employer: Is this an employment contract? How many hours a week? How is pay worked out? Is CPF payable? What is the notice period?

Common questions

Does part-time mean the same as freelance?

No. Part-time staff are employees on a contract of service, whereas freelancers are self-employed.

Is a remote part-time job possible?

Yes. Hours and location are independent of each other, so a role can be part-time and remote, or full-time and on-site.

Key takeaways

  1. A job ad can describe hours, place and status, and these are three different things.
  2. In Singapore, part-time means contracted for fewer than 35 hours a week.
  3. Freelancers look after their own CPF and tax.
  4. Ask about hours, how pay is worked out, CPF, contract type and notice period.
