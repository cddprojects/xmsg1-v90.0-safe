How to Ask Your Employer for Flexible Work, Step by Step

Last reviewed November 2026

If you are already employed and would like fewer hours, some days at home or different timings, you can put in a formal request. Good preparation improves your odds.

Be clear about the type of request

  • Flexi-place: working from home or elsewhere for part or all of the time.
  • Flexi-time: starting and finishing at different times.
  • Flexi-load: working fewer hours, as in part-time work or job-sharing.

The steps

  1. Read your contract and any company policy.
  2. Decide what you want, for how long, and from what date.
  3. Explain how the work will still be done, including any handover.
  4. Propose a trial period with a review date.
  5. Put the request in writing.
  6. Follow up politely if you hear nothing.

What to expect in return

National guidelines say employers should look at requests fairly, reply in writing within a set time and give reasons for any refusal. The MOM website has the current details.

A request doesn't guarantee a yes. A clear, practical proposal with a trial period gives you the best chance.

Common questions

Is a refusal possible?

Yes. An employer may decline, though it is expected to give its reasons in writing. The MOM guidelines have the detail.

Is it worth proposing a trial?

Yes. A trial with a review date can make it easier for an employer to agree.

Key takeaways

  1. Employees are able to make a formal flexible work arrangement request.
  2. Be precise about what you want, why you want it and from when.
  3. Suggest a trial period to make agreement easier.
  4. Employers should answer in writing and give reasons for a refusal.
