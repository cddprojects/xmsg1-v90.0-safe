Upskilling in Singapore: Funding, Courses and What Changes at Work

Last reviewed November 2026

Training can lead somewhere new, provided it connects to jobs you can actually see advertised.

CreditSkillsFuture Credit for eligible citizens
Mid-careerextra support; check SkillsFuture for current amounts
Modularcourses built for working adults

Work backwards from the job

Choose two or three ads for roles you would like, note the skills they list, then look for courses that close the gaps.

Funding worth checking

SkillsFuture Credit, mid-career support and course subsidies can all cut costs. Because amounts and eligibility are revised from time to time, check SkillsFuture before paying.

Short courses and part-time study

Plenty of programmes are modular and run in the evenings or online, which works well if you already have a part-time job.

Steer clear of

  • Courses that are not approved.
  • Any programme that promises you a job or an income.

Thinking about returning to work? See going back to work after a break.

Common questions

Who is eligible for SkillsFuture Credit?

Eligible Singapore citizens can use it, and mid-career workers get extra support. Amounts and eligibility change, so the SkillsFuture website has the latest details.

Is it possible to study and work part-time?

Yes. Plenty of courses are modular and part-time, built for working adults.

Key takeaways

  1. SkillsFuture Credit and course subsidies can bring down the cost of training.
  2. Choose training tied to a role you can see advertised.
  3. Short, stackable courses suit people who work part-time as well.
  4. Make sure a course is approved before you pay for it.
