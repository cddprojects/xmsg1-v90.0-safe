Part-time jobs

Part-time jobs in Singapore: roles, hours and what to check

A part-time job often sits alongside study, a day job or caring duties. What matters most is practical: how shifts are scheduled, what the pay works out to after travel, and how much notice you get when plans change.

Getting started

5 steps.

  1. 1

    Map out when you are free

    Write down the days and time slots you can commit to every week, travel included. Dependability matters more than squeezing in the most hours.

  2. 2

    Put together a one-page CV

    Highlight reliability, availability and any experience dealing with customers. One page is plenty.

  3. 3

    Look in the right places

    Check MyCareersFuture and the careers pages of employers you recognise, and read each listing in full, hours and pay included.

  4. 4

    Pin down the terms

    Before accepting, confirm the hours, pay rate, pay day, CPF and leave. Ask for these in writing.

  5. 5

    Review after a few weeks

    See whether the schedule still suits you, and speak up early if it does not.

Before you say yes

Points worth checking.

Rostering

Find out how early shifts are released and how swaps are handled. School holidays, festive periods and sales often mean extra or longer shifts.

How you are paid

Check whether pay is hourly or monthly, when it arrives and how public holiday work is treated. Hold on to your payslips.

CPF and leave

Ask for written confirmation that CPF is paid from month one, and how leave is calculated pro rata.

Your main job

Look for outside-work clauses in your existing contract before committing. Work pass holders should check with MOM first.

Questions to ask

  • Are the weekly hours fixed, or rostered?
  • What is the pay rate, and which day of the month is pay day?
  • Is this an employment contract that includes CPF?
  • How much warning will I get if a shift changes?
  • How long is probation, and what notice applies?
  • Who should I speak to if my availability changes?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Saying yes before you know the hours
  • Not checking whether pay is hourly or monthly
  • Taking on more than you can sustain
  • Overlooking restrictions in your main job's contract
FAQ

Common questions.

What counts as part-time in Singapore?

If you are contracted for fewer than 35 hours a week, you are a part-time employee under the part-time employment rules.

Is leave available to part-timers?

Generally yes, calculated pro rata. The MOM website sets out the current rules.

Can I hold a part-time job as well as a full-time one?

Often you can, but check the full-time contract first because some employers require approval for outside work.
