How to Read Singapore's Labour Market Figures

Last reviewed November 2026

A headline squeezes a lot into a few words. This is what the main labour numbers really measure.

Unemploymentshare of the labour force looking for work
Vacanciesopenings reported by employers
Median wagethe middle value, not the average

The unemployment rate

It tells you how many people are looking for work. In Singapore it is low, but it says nothing about underemployment or skills mismatches.

Job vacancies

This figure counts the openings that employers report, which is not the same as the number of ads you see online.

Wages

A median shows the middle of the range. What any one person earns depends on their experience and employer.

A quick routine

  • Skim the summary of the latest MOM report.
  • Look up the sector table for your field.
  • Set it against recent listings.

You might also like which sectors are hiring.

Common questions

Why do official vacancies differ from what I see online?

Official figures count the vacancies employers report, while online listings may be duplicated or already filled.

Where can I find the official tables?

The MOM Manpower Research and Statistics pages and SingStat publish them.

Key takeaways

  1. Unemployment and vacancy figures describe different things.
  2. Look at your own sector and role, not only the headline number.
  3. A wage figure is a median, not a promise.
  4. Begin with official publications and treat commentary as secondary.
