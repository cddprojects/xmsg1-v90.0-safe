Where Singapore's Hiring Is Growing: A Sector Guide

Last reviewed November 2026

The job market in Singapore doesn't head in a single direction. A few sectors keep expanding, others are being reshaped, and the work inside each is shifting fast. This is the overall picture.

5sectors named most often: healthcare, technology, finance, professional services and logistics
Quarterlyhow often MOM releases labour market reports
35 hrsthe dividing line between part-time and full-time

Healthcare and care services

Because the population is ageing, there is steady demand for nurses, allied health professionals, and eldercare and community care staff. Many of these jobs offer part-time or shift-based hours.

Technology and digital

Data, cybersecurity, cloud and AI-related skills feature repeatedly in national skills reports. Contract and remote working are common in this area.

Finance and professional services

Employers still need people in compliance, risk, analytics and advisory work, and more of them now offer hybrid schedules.

Logistics, engineering and the green economy

Supply chain, advanced manufacturing and sustainability jobs are part of Singapore's industry transformation plans.

Where that leaves you

If you are planning a full career change, or a part-time job to sit alongside your current one, focus on the skills behind a sector instead of the title on the door. Those skills travel further than most people assume.

These figures are updated every quarter, so check the latest MOM and Workforce Singapore publications listed below.

Common questions

Where are the newest sector figures published?

The Ministry of Manpower releases labour market reports each quarter, and Workforce Singapore posts updates on industry transformation.

If a sector is growing, is it easy to get hired?

Not always. How competitive it is depends on the role and on the skills and experience asked for.

Key takeaways

  1. Official labour reports most often point to healthcare, technology, finance, professional services and logistics.
  2. Growth is patchy: certain roles are hard to fill, while others draw crowds of applicants.
  3. Before settling on a direction, read the latest quarterly MOM report.
  4. Skills move between sectors more easily than job titles do.
