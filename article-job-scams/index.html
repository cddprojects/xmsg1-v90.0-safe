Seven Warning Signs of a Fake Remote Job Offer

Last reviewed November 2026

Scammers favour part-time and remote work because the offer of easy, flexible income is so tempting. These are the signs to look for.

1. You didn't apply

A job offer that arrives unprompted on WhatsApp, Telegram or SMS is a serious red flag. Genuine employers seldom message strangers cold with pay figures.

2. Pay that doesn't fit the work

Earning hundreds of dollars a day to "like" videos, boost ratings or finish simple tasks is not how real businesses pay.

3. Money has to go in first

Deposits, "training fees", equipment charges and top-ups to "unlock" tasks are exactly how scammers take your money.

4. The interview is chat-only

There is no company email, no video call, no named hiring manager, and no verifiable business in ACRA records.

5. Pressure and secrecy

Lines like "reply within the hour", "tell no one" and "only a few slots left" are meant to stop you checking.

6. Requests for sensitive details

Don't hand over your Singpass, OTPs, banking logins or a full photo of your NRIC before you hold a verified offer. Your bank account could end up being used for money mule activity.

7. It isn't on official channels

See whether the employer and the role show up on MyCareersFuture or on the company's official website.

If you have already engaged with them: stop all payments, save screenshots, call your bank and contact the Police. For advice, you can call the ScamShield helpline on 1799.

Common questions

What if I have already replied?

Stop replying, send no money, save screenshots and talk to your bank. You can call the ScamShield helpline on 1799 for advice.

How do I confirm a job exists?

Find the role on the company's own website or on MyCareersFuture, then contact the company using details you found yourself.

Key takeaways

  1. Real employers never ask you to pay before you start.
  2. Be wary of unsolicited chat-app offers and pay that looks too good.
  3. Check the company and the listing on official sources.
  4. Don't share your Singpass, OTP or bank logins. Call 1799 if in doubt.
