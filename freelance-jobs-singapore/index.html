Getting started

6 steps.

  1. 1

    Pick a niche

    Concentrate on one or two services that you do well and can describe in a single sentence.

  2. 2

    Assemble a portfolio

    Two or three strong examples are enough to begin with.

  3. 3

    Set your pricing

    Charge by the hour or by the project, and allow for revisions, admin and tax.

  4. 4

    Look for clients

    Try referrals, your own network, online platforms and professional profiles.

  5. 5

    Write everything down

    A written agreement should cover the scope, deadlines, revisions and payment terms.

  6. 6

    Keep a record of income

    Log every payment, because freelance income has to be declared.

Before you say yes

Points worth checking.

Contract for services

Set out the scope, deadline, revisions and payment terms on paper. For bigger projects, think about asking for a deposit.

Tax

Freelance earnings are taxable and must be declared to IRAS. Keep records of what comes in and goes out.

MediSave

Depending on income, self-employed people may have to contribute to MediSave. CPF Board has the details.

Business registration

Depending on how you trade, you might need to register a business with ACRA. ACRA and IRAS can advise.

Questions to ask

  • What does the project cover, exactly?
  • How many rounds of revisions are included?
  • What is the payment schedule, and when is each payment due?
  • Who owns the finished work?
  • Is a deposit needed?
  • How are changes to the scope handled?

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting without a written agreement
  • Pricing too low and forgetting admin time
  • Neglecting to record income for tax
  • Juggling more clients than you can handle
FAQ

Common questions.

Is employer CPF paid to freelancers?

No. Freelancers get no employer CPF contribution, though self-employed people may need to pay into MediSave, depending on income. CPF Board has current information.

Must I register a business before freelancing?

That depends on how you work. ACRA and IRAS can tell you what applies to your case.

Where do freelancers in Singapore find clients?

Typical sources are referrals, professional networks and online platforms.
